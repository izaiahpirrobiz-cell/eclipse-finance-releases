01 Download

Your books never leave your computer.

Every other bookkeeping app keeps your accounts on somebody else's server. Eclipse Finance keeps them in a file on your disk. It reads the statement files your bank already gives you, reconciles against them, and tells you what the balance looks like six weeks out.

Other platforms

Before you run it

These builds are not code signed yet. Windows will say the publisher is unrecognised — choose More info, then Run anyway. On a Mac, right-click the app and choose Open the first time.

That warning means nobody has vouched for the publisher yet. It is not a claim that anything is wrong with the app, and we would rather say so here than have you find out from a red dialog.

02 What it does

Built for the small business that does its own books.

It reads your bank's own files

Download the file your bank offers for Quicken — .ofx, .qfx or .qbo — and it imports directly. No column mapping, no spreadsheet surgery.

Those files carry the bank's own transaction ids, so importing the same statement twice adds nothing the second time. It suggests categories from how you have filed the same places before, and flags anything that merely looks like a duplicate instead of deciding for you.

Tick your statement off, line by line

The opening balance carries from the last statement, so each one continues the one before and the chain cannot be quietly re-based.

It only closes when the difference is zero — or when you knowingly write a balancing entry, which appears in the register as a real transaction rather than hiding in a fudge column. Reconciled lines then lock, so the numbers cannot change behind you.

Know what is coming before it leaves

Record a bill when it arrives, not when you pay it. Nothing moves until you press Pay — but from that moment it counts against your forecast.

Aging buckets show how late everything is, because "we are sixty days out on that" means something specific to both sides of a supplier call.

A register that reads like a statement

Date on the left, amount on the right, one line per transaction, categories editable in place. Filter by date, category, amount or whether a line has been reconciled — and the total updates with it.

Attach the receipt to the line it belongs to. The file is copied into your books, so tidying your Downloads folder later cannot empty the evidence behind a tax return.

03 Why that is safer

The safest place for your accounts is the one nobody else can reach.

There is no account to breach

You do not sign up to anything. Your books are a file on your disk, protected by your computer's own account, and no server anywhere holds a copy. A company that never had your numbers cannot lose them, be subpoenaed for them, or sell them.

Backups that actually come back

One file holds your ledger and every receipt, each with a checksum. A damaged backup is refused rather than half-restored, and restoring one sets your current books aside instead of overwriting them — so the wrong backup is not the last mistake you get to make.

Numbers that cannot change quietly

Once a line is reconciled it is locked, and editing it means undoing that statement first. Imported statements match on the bank's own transaction ids, and anything that merely looks like a duplicate is flagged for you rather than decided for you.

Everything that leaves the machine

The whole list, so you can check it yourself.

Checking for updates
Asks GitHub whether a newer version exists. Sends nothing about you.
Payment processors
Only if you connect Stripe, Square or PayPal yourself. Your keys live in your operating system's keychain, never in the app's own files.
The invitation list
During the private alpha, your email address when you sign in, to check you were invited.
Nothing else. Ever.
No analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting, no "anonymous usage data". Your transactions, balances, customers and receipts are never transmitted anywhere.
04 Security, specifically

The details, so you can judge them yourself.

Your password
Hashed with scrypt at N=215, r=8 — deliberately slow and memory-hard, so guessing at it costs real hardware. Compared in constant time, so how long a wrong answer takes reveals nothing about how close it was.
Payment processor keys
Encrypted by your operating system's own keychain — DPAPI on Windows, Keychain on macOS, libsecret on Linux — and tied to your OS user account. Where no keychain exists, they fall back to AES-256-GCM under your passphrase. Plaintext keys are held only long enough to sign one request and never reach the part of the app that draws the screen.
Your recovery code
160 bits from the system random source, shown once, and stored only as a hash. It is single use: recovering with it clears it, because a recovery code left live is a second permanent key to your books.
Backups
Every file inside a backup carries a SHA-256 checksum. A damaged or truncated archive is refused rather than half-restored, and a restore stages everything and verifies it before touching what you have — then sets the old copy aside rather than deleting it.
Your books, at rest
A database file in your user folder, protected by your operating system account like every other file you own. Not encrypted — see below.
Telemetry
None. No analytics, no crash reporting, no usage pings. The only outbound requests are the three listed above, and you can verify that with any network monitor.

What this does not do

Stated here rather than left for you to discover.

The ledger file is not encrypted at rest
Anyone who can reach your unlocked computer can read it, the same as any document on your disk. Full-disk encryption — BitLocker or FileVault — is the right answer today, and at-rest encryption is on the list.
The builds are not code signed yet
Which is why Windows warns that the publisher is unrecognised and macOS wants a right-click then Open. It also means you are trusting the download itself; every build is published from a public, reproducible CI run rather than from anybody's laptop.
The invitation gate is a gate, not a lock
It stops an installer being casually passed around during the alpha. It runs on your computer, so somebody determined could remove it — and gain nothing but the use of an app they were not invited to, since no server holds anyone's books.

06 Questions

The things worth asking first.

What does it cost?

Nothing, during the alpha. There is no subscription and no account, so there is nothing to cancel if you stop using it.

Can I try it right now?

It is invite-only while it is an alpha. Ask for an invitation and you will be added to the list.

Where exactly are my books kept?

In a single database file in your user folder, alongside a folder of any receipts you attach. Settings shows you the exact path, and opens it.

Is the file encrypted?

No — it is protected by your computer's user account, like every other file on your disk. Payment-processor keys are encrypted, in your OS keychain. We would rather say this plainly than imply more.

What if I stop using it?

Export the register to CSV, or take a backup. Nothing is held hostage, because nothing of yours is anywhere we control.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Everything except syncing a payment processor and checking for updates works with no connection at all.

Which banks does it support?

Any bank that lets you download a statement — .ofx, .qfx and .qbo import directly, and CSV works with a quick column check. There is no bank connection to break.

Personal or business?

Both, and they are kept separate. A business book gets invoices, customers and payment links; a personal one gets net worth instead and is not cluttered with the rest.
